Dispatches

Occasional notes on what changed and why. Written for one reader.

The portfolio is up 8.7% on the year and every position is doing roughly what it was hired to do. The temptation in stretches like this is to tinker, trim NVDA, chase something newer. Resist it. The single biggest driver of long-run results in this account is time in market, not adjustments.

The one exception worth acting on: US equity has drifted 4 points above target. New contributions are already routed to VXUS and BND to close the gap by October without triggering a taxable sale.

SCHD lifted its quarterly distribution 9% year over year, which pushes projected annual income past $11,600. The point of this sleeve was never yield-chasing, it's a stream that grows through drawdowns so the plan never depends on selling shares at a bad moment. It's doing that.

The portfolio fell 9.8% peak-to-trough in February and recovered fully by mid-April. Two things worked: the bond sleeve gained while equities fell, and contributions kept buying through the bottom. The lesson isn't that drawdowns are painless, it's that the account is built so you never have to act during one.

Learn

Short answers to questions this portfolio actually raises.

Bonds aren't in the portfolio to maximize return, they're there so February-style drawdowns are −10% instead of −18%, and so there's dry powder to rebalance with. The cost is a bit of upside; the payment is being able to stay invested.

Instead of selling winners (and paying capital gains tax) to restore target weights, new money is directed to whatever is underweight. It's slower than selling, but in a taxable account it's usually worth the wait.

Most of the equity dividends (VTI, SCHD, MSFT, AAPL) are qualified, taxed at the lower capital-gains rate. BND's interest and SGOV's Treasury income are ordinary income, though SGOV is exempt from state tax. VNQ's distributions are mostly ordinary, which is why REIT exposure is kept modest in a taxable account.

It's at the high end of comfortable. The working rule for this account: no single name above 12%, the three combined below 25%. NVDA crossing 6% of the portfolio would trigger a trim under those rules, it's at 5.3% today.
